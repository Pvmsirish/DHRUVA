How to play

Four keys, one rule: don't eat yourself.

Viper runs on a fixed grid. Every tick, the snake advances exactly one cell in its current direction — there's no coasting, no diagonal shortcuts, and no forgiveness for clipping a wall or your own tail. Steering is queued, not instant: press a direction and it takes effect on the next tick, so the snake never reverses into itself even if you mash the keys.

  • Move with the arrow keys or WASD on desktop, or the on-screen pad on touch devices.
  • Eat the glowing apple to grow one segment and add ten points.
  • Every five apples, the tick interval shortens and the whole board visibly quickens.
  • Pause any time with Space or the Pause button — the board freezes exactly where it stands.
  • Crash into a wall or your own body and the run ends; your score is compared against your saved high score instantly.

The speed curve

Five apples buys you one gear.

Viper starts at a deliberately forgiving pace so the controls have time to feel natural. From then on, every fifth apple multiplies the tick interval by 0.88, which compounds fast: by apple twenty the board is moving nearly twice as quickly as it started, and by apple thirty-five you're at the game's speed floor, where every millisecond of reaction time matters.

The curve is intentionally asymptotic rather than linear — it never reaches zero, so late-game runs stay tense but never literally unplayable. Choosing Hard mode in the starting-speed selector simply moves you further along this same curve before the run even begins, for players who find the first five apples too easy to take seriously.

Strategy notes

Playing for score, not just survival

New players tend to chase the apple in a straight line and worry about the walls later; that habit is exactly what ends most runs once the board speeds up. A better default is to think one lap ahead: before committing to a direction, glance at where your tail currently sits, because at higher speeds a single mistimed turn is enough to trap you against your own body with nowhere left to go.

Hugging the edges is tempting because it feels safe, but it quietly removes your escape routes. A snake that plays close to the center of the board always has at least two directions open; a snake pinned to a wall only ever has one. Treat the outer ring of cells as a place to pass through, not a place to live.

Once the interval starts shortening past the third or fourth speed-up, resist the urge to take the shortest path to the apple. Instead, favor the path that leaves your tail curled into open space rather than into a corner. Sacrificing a slightly longer route in exchange for a safer shape is almost always worth the extra half-second, because the game rewards apples eaten in total, not apples eaten quickly.

Finally, use Pause deliberately. It costs nothing, freezes the tick accumulator exactly where it stands, and is the single most under-used tool available to you — a two-second breath before a tight turn at top speed is often the difference between a new high score and a restart.

About this build

One file, built to last

Viper is a single self-contained HTML file: markup, design tokens, and game logic all live together with no build step and no external dependencies. That was a deliberate constraint — it means the game keeps working exactly as-is for as long as browsers exist, with nothing to install and nothing that can go out of date.

  • Grid engine

    Movement runs on a fixed-timestep accumulator driven by requestAnimationFrame, so the game speed is tied to real elapsed time, not frame rate.

  • Speed ramp

    Every fifth apple shortens the tick interval by a fixed multiplier, with separate floors for Easy, Normal, and Hard.

  • Persistence layer

    High score and run history are written to localStorage immediately when a run ends, before the game-over overlay even renders.

  • Interface pass

    Theme tokens, focus states, a touch d-pad, and a full responsive pass were added so the same file works from a phone to a widescreen monitor.
